# Recommendations Memo
**To:** Marketing & Customer Retention Leadership  
**From:** Analytics Team  
**Date:** Q3 2024  
**Re:** Customer Retention Priorities — Q4 Action Plan

---

This memo summarises the findings from a two-year analysis of our customer transaction data and translates them into four prioritised actions for the coming quarter. No technical background is needed to act on these recommendations.

---

## The Headline Finding

Our business follows a stark concentration pattern: **22% of our customers — roughly 1,300 people — are responsible for 68% of our total revenue.** These are our "Champions": frequent buyers with high average spend (£9,100 each over two years). Everything else follows from this.

The second finding is equally important: **only 1 in 5 customers ever makes a second purchase.** The moment someone places their first order is our single biggest window to convert a one-time buyer into a repeat customer — and right now, we are missing 80% of them.

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Launch a Win-Back Campaign for At-Risk High-Value Customers — *This Quarter*
**Priority: URGENT**

There is a segment of 1,754 customers — our "Loyal Customers" — who historically purchased regularly (averaging nearly 6 orders) but whose last purchase was, on average, **6 months ago**. Collectively they represent **£4.1 million in historical spend** (23% of total revenue).

These customers are not gone — they are drifting. A targeted, personalised reactivation campaign referencing their purchase history (e.g., "It's been a while since your last order of [product category]") is likely to perform 3–5x better than a generic promotional email.

**Projected impact:** If a focused campaign reactivates 15% of this segment at their historical average order value, that represents approximately **£350,000 in recovered revenue** this quarter.

**Recommended action:** Brief the CRM team this week. Campaign should be in market within 3 weeks — before the Q4 Christmas build-up begins.

---

### 2. Protect Our Champions with a Loyalty Programme — *Before End of Q4*
**Priority: HIGH**

Our 1,300 Champion customers generate £11.9 million in revenue. Losing even 10% of them would cost approximately **£1.2 million annually** — more than the entire revenue contribution of every other segment combined.

These customers do not need discounts — they already buy without price incentives. What they respond to is recognition: early access to new products, a named loyalty tier, exclusive previews. A lightweight VIP programme costs far less than replacing these customers through acquisition.

**Recommended action:** Design a two-tier loyalty structure (e.g., "Silver" for Loyal Customers, "Gold" for Champions). Roll out before the November peak so Champions feel rewarded during the highest-spend month of the year.

---

### 3. Fix the First-Purchase Experience — *Ongoing, Start Immediately*
**Priority: HIGH**

The data shows a brutal truth: **79% of first-time buyers never return.** This is not primarily a product problem — it is an onboarding and follow-up problem. New customers who do not receive a meaningful touchpoint within their first 30 days almost universally lapse.

A structured 3-email welcome sequence — (1) order confirmation + brand story on day 0, (2) product care or usage tips on day 7, (3) personalised "customers like you also bought" recommendation on day 21 — has strong evidence in retail of lifting second-purchase conversion by 8–15 percentage points.

**Projected impact:** Improving first-to-second purchase conversion from 21% to 30% across the ~300 new customers acquired each month would add approximately **270 additional retained customers per year**, each with an estimated lifetime value of £500–£1,300.

**Recommended action:** Approve a 3-email welcome sequence. This requires no budget beyond CRM platform time — the content can be drafted in one week.

---

### 4. Plan Q4 Inventory and Staffing Around the November Peak — *Confirm Plans Now*
**Priority: MEDIUM (time-sensitive)**

Revenue analysis shows a consistent and significant demand spike in October–November each year, driven by Christmas gifting. November generates roughly **£728,000 more than an average month**. Our Q4 revenue forecast projects January–March 2024 at £400,000–£480,000 per month — a significant step-down from November's peak.

**Recommended action:** Confirm that supplier purchase orders for Q4 gifting stock are already placed (lead times of 6–8 weeks mean September is the last viable moment). Staffing plans for warehouse and customer service should reflect that November will require approximately double the capacity of January.

---

## What We Are Not Recommending

We are *not* recommending broad acquisition campaigns at this time. Customer acquisition costs have risen, and the data shows our retention problem is larger than our acquisition problem. Filling the top of the funnel while the bottom leaks is an expensive strategy. Fixing retention first will produce a higher return on every pound spent on acquisition later.

---

*This memo is based on transaction data from Dec 2009 – Dec 2011. Projections are directional estimates. Actual results will depend on campaign execution quality and market conditions.*
